BEFORE YOU REVIVE A DOMAIN

Run these checks before you commit.

01

Confirm registration status

Open the current RDAP record and a registrar's search page. A previous feed entry can be out of date.

02

Review its website history

Use the Wayback Machine to inspect older pages, topics and redirects. Check several dates rather than relying on one capture.

03

Investigate reputation and rights

Search for spam, malware, trademark conflicts, previous abuse and backlink quality before acquiring or rebuilding.

UNDERSTANDING THE DATA

Research signals, not guarantees.

What should I search first?

Start with the dropped-domain feed if you are looking for names that have already left the registry. Use expired domains to track names that may drop later, and treat DNS-removed names as a weaker observation that needs more investigation.

Does an expired domain mean I can register it?

No. It may still be in a grace period, redemption or pending-delete stage and can remain registered to its current holder. Check the live RDAP record and registrar status.

What does the name score mean?

It is a small, explainable heuristic based on name length, letters, digits, hyphens and selected familiar extensions. It is not an appraisal and says nothing about traffic, backlinks, revenue or search demand.

Why is the dataset incomplete?

The public WhoisFreaks GitHub feed is a limited free sample rather than its full daily database. TeamPeach also depends on the source publishing a usable snapshot. Some source datasets may be unavailable on a given day.

Does a missing Wayback capture mean the domain was never used?

No. The Internet Archive may not have crawled the site, may have incomplete captures or may exclude some pages. Archive history is useful evidence, not a complete record.

Does TeamPeach buy or register domains for me?

No. This tool helps you research candidates. Registration, purchase negotiation, trademark review and rebuilding are separate decisions you must complete with the relevant providers.
